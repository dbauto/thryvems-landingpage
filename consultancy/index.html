CONSULTANCY

Management system consultancy areas

Compare ISO management-system subjects before agreeing on a scoped implementation project.

Choose the right project

A consultancy discussion should begin with your activities, organizational responsibilities, sites, customers and requirements. Define the standard and edition, processes in scope, intended work products and what the company will do internally. The ISO consultancy guide explains how to compare engagements, deliverables and the separation between consultancy and independent certification. Avoid assuming one fixed implementation package fits every organization.

Quality, environment and safety

ISO 9001 concerns quality processes, customer requirements, suppliers and performance. ISO 14001 addresses environmental aspects and obligations. ISO 45001 covers occupational health and safety hazards and controls. Each area requires its own evidence, responsible people and technical understanding. A consultant may support assessment and improvement, while the business remains responsible for using its management system.

Security, food safety and certification

ISO/IEC 27001 considers information security risks and controls. ISO 22000 covers food safety hazards, operating controls and verification. Independent certification bodies—not consultants—decide whether to issue certificates. Discuss scope, training, audit stages, fees and surveillance separately. The quotation guide helps identify these components. The developing ThryveMS software does not issue certificates.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from your consultancy guide?

This page organizes standard-specific service areas; the detailed guide discusses consultancy deliverables.

Can consultancy issue certificates?

No. Independent certification bodies make those decisions.

Which standards have detailed guides?

ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 45001, ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 22000.

Can staff training be part of a consultancy project?

It can be included if agreed in the project scope.

What should a proposal specify?

Covered sites, responsibilities, deliverables, milestones and separate certification fees.
