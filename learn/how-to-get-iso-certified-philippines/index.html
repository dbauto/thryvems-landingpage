ISO certification guide

How to get ISO certified in the Philippines

If your organization has been asked for ISO certification by a customer or procurement team, begin by clarifying the exact standard and scope. Certification is an independent assessment of a management system. It is not a government business registration, not a permit and not simply a document package purchased from a consultant. The process is easier to manage when each decision is made in the right order.

1. Confirm which ISO standard is actually required

The phrase “ISO certified” is incomplete because ISO publishes many different standards. ISO 9001 covers quality management; ISO 14001 covers environmental management; ISO 45001 addresses worker health and safety; ISO/IEC 27001 addresses information security; ISO 22000 concerns food safety management. The correct choice depends on business activities and contractual or other relevant requirements.

For example, a supplier may be asked for ISO 9001 by a buyer, while another customer requests an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate because the supplier processes sensitive information. Read the written requirement carefully. Ask whether it specifies the edition, permitted accreditation, certificate scope or a deadline.

2. Define the sites, products and processes

Certification covers an agreed management system scope. List activities, branches, offices, outsourced operations and services that should be included. The scope must be truthful and usable: a certificate for a small administrative unit may not cover manufacturing or site work performed elsewhere.

For a company with a head office and multiple operational locations, clarify which controls are central and which need local implementation. Document exclusions only where the selected standard permits them and the decision is justified.

3. Review what the business already does

A gap assessment compares existing policies, processes and records with relevant requirements. Many businesses already control purchasing, complaints, training, risks or operational instructions in some form. The task is to identify what works and what is missing, not to create a second system of paperwork for an auditor.

Arrange findings into a manageable plan with owners and due dates. Include the work needed to identify applicable laws, contracts and other obligations relevant to the organization's management system. A consultant may guide the analysis, but management must approve the scope and resources.

4. Implement processes and generate evidence

Agree on process responsibilities, document controls, objectives, measurement and corrective action. Train people to follow the system and make it practical for their roles. Evidence might include approved instructions, supplier assessments, service records, incident reports, operational checks and customer feedback, depending on the standard.

Avoid pretending that evidence exists by backdating records. Certification auditors need to see a management system being operated over time. A staged implementation may be more effective than releasing all procedures on the same day and expecting immediate compliance.

5. Audit internally and review management performance

Conduct an internal audit against the applicable requirements and the organization's own controls. Record what the auditors checked and the evidence used. Where findings arise, investigate causes, act and verify effectiveness. Internal auditors should have suitable competence and independence.

Top management then reviews whether the management system remains suitable, adequate and effective. That review should consider performance, customer and stakeholder information, risks, resources, audit findings and improvement opportunities. These activities should inform business decisions, not exist solely to produce an audit record.

6. Choose an independent certification body

ISO itself does not issue management system certificates. Ask certification bodies about their accreditation, audit scope, auditor experience, stage-one and stage-two arrangements, decision process, surveillance audits and total contract costs. In the Philippines, the DTI Philippine Accreditation Bureau is a relevant reference when evaluating accreditation.

For a PhilGEPS procurement opportunity, read the bid documents rather than assuming every government tender requires ISO certification. Some tenders specify certification-body accreditation and scope. Verify the procurement requirements with the procuring entity and prospective certification body before spending money.

What help can a consultant provide?

A consultant can help a team interpret the relevant management system requirements, identify gaps and prepare its own procedures and audit evidence. The client remains responsible for operating the system. Only the independent certification body decides whether to award a management system certificate; consultancy fees and audit fees should be stated separately. For a scope-based proposal, compare what the consultant will deliver, which sites are involved, and what the selected certification body will review. See our ISO consultancy guide for questions to ask before engaging a provider.

7. Complete certification and keep improving

If the certification body determines that requirements have been met, it may issue a certificate for the audited scope. Findings may need correction before the decision. After certification, the system still needs internal audits, management reviews, controlled records, training and follow-up.

Surveillance and recertification are part of continuing certification arrangements. Changes to locations, operations, ownership or significant processes should be discussed with the certification body where relevant. A certificate is not a permanent substitute for operating an effective management system.

Frequently asked questions

Can my company buy an ISO certificate?

A legitimate accredited certification requires an independent assessment; purchasing templates does not meet that requirement.

How many months should we allow?

It depends on scope, existing controls, available people and audit scheduling.

Is certification issued by DTI?

Certification is issued by third-party certification bodies; DTI's Philippine Accreditation Bureau relates to accreditation.

Can one certificate cover several sites?

Potentially, depending on the defined scope and certification-body rules.

Can we prepare without software?

Yes. Software is optional and should support, not replace, a working management system.

Is ISO 9001:2026 the current quality management edition?

Yes. ISO published that edition in September 2026.
